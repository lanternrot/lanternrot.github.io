"Thirty years. Not one has passed." Oskar took the watch on a frontier wall the kingdom soon forgot it had built, and he has been waiting on a relief column ever since. In 4.12.0 he finally gets someone to stand in front of. He binds one ally to himself with the cold and takes their blows on his own frozen plate, and the Watch finally pays him for it.

He isn't the only one keeping score. Every monster that lays a hand on your party now carries a cracked shield for it, and the heroes who hold a grudge can see exactly who has it coming.

## The Cold Link

Hold the Line is now a Frost Guardian Link. Pick another ally and Oskar ties himself to them for their next five turns. Part of every blow meant for them lands on him instead, and the cold in his armor turns half of that away. Cast it again and the Link moves to whoever needs it now.

He's at his best when the monsters pick a victim and stay on them. A single huge swing is still a job for his Icebound Oath. The Link is for the ally who gets hunted round after round.

His new capstone, Back Pay, keeps him in business. The damage his Shields soak and his Link takes off an ally earns him MP back, so a Paladin who spends his turns protecting people can afford to keep doing it.

Two Links on one ally don't add up. If an ally already holds another guardian's Link that is as strong or stronger, Hold the Line isn't offered on them, so you never pay for a Link that would do nothing. Aurora's and "Ninety-Nine" Dask's Links follow the same rule.

## The cracked shield

A few heroes hit harder at whoever hit them first. Oskar's Watchman's Strike lays extra Frost on a monster that struck him or someone he protects. Fennick's Low Blow blinds a monster that went after him, and his Pin Down stuns it. Until now you had to remember who did what.

Now, while you aim one of those skills, every monster it would pay off on carries a cracked steel shield at the end of its health bar. The skill's page in the book spells out the bonus. The hero's token in the turn order wears the same shield whenever they're ready to answer a hit, so you can plan for it before their turn comes up.

Rosara's Quiet Defiance and Sir Gallan's Defensive Footwork wait on the hero being hit at all, whoever swung, so they show the token mark alone.

## The map follows you

After a win, the map opens on the node you just beat and holds there a moment. Then it glides to the node that just opened, easing off and easing in, while your boot prints walk the new trail ahead of it. An event choice that opens a road does the same from wherever the camera sits.

Touch the map to stop the glide early. The prints keep walking. With Reduce Transitions on in Settings, or reduced motion on your phone, the map skips straight there.

## Hero changes

### Oskar of the Bitter Watch

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold the Line | Links one ally for 3 turns: 30% of the damage dealt to them goes to Oskar. Oskar takes 15% less damage for 3 turns (it wore off after 2). 22 MP | Links one other ally for their next 5 turns: 40% of the damage dealt to them goes to Oskar, and he takes only half of it. Oskar takes 15% less damage for 4 turns. Casting it again moves the Link. 20 MP |
| Bitter Wind | 1 Frost on every enemy. Oskar takes 10% less damage for 2 turns (it wore off after 1) | 1 Frost on every enemy. Oskar takes 10% less damage for 2 turns. It doesn't add to other damage reduction he has; the larger one counts |
| Frozen Fortification | Every third hit that costs Oskar HP puts 1 Frost on every enemy | The same, except damage he takes through his Link doesn't count |
| Capstone | The Bitter Cold: when Oskar takes part of a hit through his Link, the attacker gets 1 Frost | Back Pay: Oskar recovers 3 MP for every 30 damage his Shields soak or his Link takes off an ally, up to 6 MP a turn |

**Why:** Hold the Line almost never got cast. In 50 test fights at Titan's Throne, Oskar used it twice and his Icebound Oath 856 times, and The Bitter Cold only did anything in that one rare case. Bitter Wind's protection ran out a turn before its text said.

## Also in this patch

- Damage numbers show what actually landed, after shields, damage reduction and redirects. A killing blow shows the whole hit.
- Every tavern game has a How to play button in its top bar that opens its Field Guide page over the game. In Fishing it works between casts. Copper Jack's Coin Toss gets a Field Guide page of its own.
- Fishing: every cast ends in a bite to strike. A fish that turns your bait down sends a common one in its place, and the catch card says so. A fish out of your line's reach tells you, and the fight ends in view.
- In Copper Jack's Coin Toss, a toss the Lucky Charm saves reads as the Charm's, on the card and in Jack's line. "Charm ate that one. She'll want it back with interest."
- The summon info sheet shows each rarity's overall rate beside its per-pull rate. Overall counts Hope and the 4★ guarantee over many pulls, so a 5★ is 2% a pull and 3.2% overall.
- Eight heroes' level-12 tales get new endings that land on the hero, where they used to stop on the name of a skill.

## Fixes

- The Challenge, Start Exploration and shrine buttons on the Genus Loci, Exploration and Shrine panels on the map show again. They had gone invisible while staying tappable.
- While you pick an ally for a skill, the other skill buttons and Back stay lit.
- The Fishing lake no longer shows through its Field Guide page.
