Six autumns ago the reapers of Bramleigh sat down to their harvest supper, and the board has not been cleared since. Every platter fills again as it empties, and no guest rises until the board is cleared. The reaping gang has been sitting there for six years.

November's banner brings you the three people at that supper whom the custom never bound. The host never sits, one guest came in after the grace, and the washer-up was hired for one evening. Ambrose serves, Quorra keeps the bill, and Wilf clears up.

## The Groaning Board

The Groaning Board runs all of November. Ambrose Wain is its 5★, Quorra Vell its 4★, and Wilf Hobday its 3★. Each is the first of their kind. Ambrose is the first Holy Druid, Quorra the first 4★ Arcane Mage, and Wilf the first Holy Ranger.

## Ambrose Wain, Who Said Grace

{% include figure.html src="ambrose_wain_tale.webp" alt="Ambrose Wain, a broad man in a gold beaked mask and a wine-red frock coat stitched with wheat, holding out a platter of roast fowl and apples, a carving knife held low behind him" side="wide" %}

> There's a place laid for you, guest. For what we are about to receive... no, have the end crust, it's the best of it.

Ambrose said the old reapers' grace over the last load, that this table never want, and it took. He is the host, and a host never sits. He knows the closing grace that lets a table rise. He has never got to the end of it.

He calls everyone guest, monsters included, and he feeds them. Food he serves an enemy lands as Holy damage that goes straight through its DEF, and leaves it Gorged. A Gorged enemy can't keep its healing down. HP it would get back from its own skills, its healers or anything it drinks is served to it as damage too, up to a share of Ambrose's ATK each round. When a Gorged enemy dies, it bursts over every other enemy.

His own heals stay on your side of the table, and they double while he's the only healer in the party. If your other healer falls, his next heal is doubled. As leader, Grace Before the Meal sits every wave down Gorged.

He's best against anything that heals itself. The Fen Leeches won't enjoy dinner, and neither will the Hydra.

## Quorra Vell, Who Came After Grace

{% include figure.html src="quorra_vell_tale.webp" alt="Quorra Vell, a big woman in a plum gown with a white jabot and pins in her hair, holding a dish of custard and a glowing wand, a long handwritten bill of fare hanging from her arm" side="wide" %}

> The custom binds a guest from the grace to the last remove. I came in after the grace, which I have confirmed twice, and so it does not bind me. Is that the custard?

Quorra kept the seating at the stewards' table of the Lists for twenty years, and nobody living knows the rules of precedence better. She came in to Bramleigh's Harvest Home just after the grace was said, so she could leave whenever she likes. She is owed every course on the bill of fare, and she has not finished.

In a fight she keeps track of what every monster has coming, and sends it back to the kitchen. Send It Back hits harder when a monster's skill is ready for its next turn, and pushes that skill back a round. Between Courses, at level 12, hits every enemy and holds back the most dangerous one's ready skill.

She also notes who helped themselves. A monster that used a skill since her last turn takes more from On the Slate and Count the Covers. On the Slate also takes Mana Strain off your party's most-strained skill, and every stack she takes pushes the monster's longest-cooldown skill back a round. She can hold any one skill back two rounds at most between its uses, so a boss still gets its turn.

Her capstone, Settling Up, runs a bill on every monster. Each skill it casts goes on the bill, and Count the Covers settles it, harder for every item on it. Bills clear between waves.

| Settling Up (capstone) | Every skill an enemy casts goes on its bill until Count the Covers hits it. Count the Covers then deals 95% for one item, 20% more for each further item, up to 135% |


## Wilf Hobday, Hired for the Evening

{% include figure.html src="wilf_hobday_tale.webp" alt="Wilf Hobday, a thin, tired man in a patched green apron, drying a plate with a striped cloth, another plate tucked under his arm" side="wide" %}

> Wilf Hobday. Hired for the evening. Where's your sink? I won't be stopping long.

Wilf was hired to wash up at Bramleigh's Harvest Home for one evening, for a shilling and his supper. Every dish he has cleared since has gone back to the board full. He handed in his notice in the second year and is working it out to the end of the evening, which has so far lasted six years.

In a fight he clears tables. He hits harder for every enemy already gone this wave, and hardest when only one is left. When the field drops to a single enemy, his quarry, One Left, gives him his Focus back. Next Out marks a target so the whole party hits it harder, and Clear Them Off finishes the ones running low.

At level 12 he learns The Last Dish, a heavy Holy hit that lands far harder on the last enemy standing. His capstone, Never Dropped One, lets him throw it without spending his Focus. He's at his best against bosses who fight alone.

| Next Out | 80% ATK damage to one enemy, which is Marked for 2 turns and takes 20% more damage from everyone |
| Clearing Up (level 3, passive) | +10% to all his damage for each enemy fallen or gone this wave, up to +30%. While only one enemy stands, +50% |
| Clear Them Off (level 6) | 120% ATK damage to one enemy, or 200% if it's at or below 40% HP |
| The Last Dish (level 12) | 150% ATK Holy damage to one enemy. Against the only enemy left, 360%, ignoring 30% of its DEF. 3-turn cooldown |
| One Left (quarry) | He regains Focus when the field drops to one enemy, by a death or a departure |
| Never Dropped One (capstone) | The Last Dish doesn't spend his Focus |

## Something's cooking in Bramleigh

From today, a new sign hangs on Home for anyone who has cleared Whispering Woods. It reads "A feast that won't end." and counts the days down to Bramleigh, which opens on November 16.

Beating the Goblin Cavern, the fight that opens the road to the Caverns, is what counts. The sign says nothing else.
