"Few have reached these heights. Fewer have returned." That's the Summit's warning, and now you can see why. It has a new map, painted at a stormy dusk, with an ice dome glowing on the central peak and a throne of black spires waiting under a red sky.

Getting up there should feel like something, and now it does. When the boss at the end of the road finally drops, the fight slows down to watch it go. This is the biggest Dorf patch yet, and most of it is about how a hard-won fight feels when you win it.

## Level up in style

When a hero levels up, the game stops for them. Their tale painting fills the screen, a Level Up title floats up over it, and the hero says a line in their own voice while their stats count up from the old level to the new one, to a fanfare. Each hero who levelled gets a scene of their own. A tap moves on, and Skip all skips the rest. Heroes who don't have a voice yet get the painting and the stats.

It plays everywhere a hero gains levels. After a win, the scenes come up over the victory screen once the loot has landed. They also play when an expedition comes home, when you feed XP on a hero's page, after a token quick-clear, and when an event or the tavern hands out XP.

Fusing a hero to a new star rank gets its own Rank Up scene. The new star stamps in beside the old ones, the glow behind the painting shifts from the old rarity's colour to the new one, and the hero has something to say about it. It plays from the Fusion screen and from the Fuse button on a hero's page.

## Boss kills land

When a boss dies, the killing blow plays in slow motion. The camera pushes in on the boss, and the music drops away for a breath before it comes back. It plays for a node's boss, every Genus Loci, a Forlorn Hope boss and the boss of the Maw. Colosseum bouts and ordinary waves go on as before.

The loot that follows has more ceremony too. Before a rare drop falls, a column of light in its rarity colour stands where it will land, and the drop waits in it for a moment, then falls straight down the beam. Gold and gems count up as they land.

Big moves are bigger. Every hero with Hero Tales now has a line before their biggest move, 41 heroes in all. The attack painting is half again as tall, wider than the screen, and it pans across the whole picture before it lunges at the enemy.

## Combat sounds, redone

The fight sounds like a fight now. Buffs go up with a quick shoosh. Heals pour like water over a harp. Spells hit with a sound of their own, and Burn, Poison and Bleed each tick with theirs. A hero who survives a killing blow at 1 HP makes a sound about it. Enemies and fallen heroes each get a death sound, and a boss goes out in a long, shaking, static-filled fade, the way old console bosses used to. The bells and the dice are new too.

A sound you hear over and over plays a little higher or lower each time, so a long fight doesn't drone. Knuckle Bess's Jab-Jab rolls out punch by punch, each one a step higher than the last.

## The daily parcel

The benefactor's daily gift used to pop up by itself a minute after you opened the game, already paid out. Now a paper parcel tied with red cord waits on the floor at your party's feet on the Home screen until you want it.

{% include art-strip.html items=page.art.parcel scale=4 scale_sm=3 caption="A parcel, and a seal day's trunk" %}

Tap it when you like. The parcel page shows which parcel this is out of 28, a note from the benefactor, the round's notches, and what the next seal day brings. Open cracks the seal, the rewards come out one at a time, and your gold and gems fly up to their counters. On parcels 7, 14, 21 and 28 an iron-banded trunk waits in its place, and it strains at its seal in a swell of gold light before it gives.

Parcels are counted as you take them, one a day. A day away costs you nothing, since the next parcel just waits for you, but missed days don't stack. After the 28th a new round starts. Your count carries over from the old gift calendar, and the Benefactor's Mark is retired now that a missed day can't cost you a gift. New players get their first parcel after their first summon.

## The Colosseum Gate fights back

The Colosseum Gate used to be a pair of dire wolves and a pair of lake serpents standing between you and the arena. Now the arena sends its own. The Gate is one fight against four of its gladiators, to the Colosseum's own battle music, so you hear the crowd before you've earned a seat.

{% include art-rows.html items=page.art.gladiators scale=2 scale_sm=2 %}

They fight as a team, and the order you take them down in matters.

## The Hydra, in one piece

{% include figure.html src="hydra.webp" alt="The Hydra, a coiled green serpent body with four heads on four necks, rising from dark water" caption="The Hydra" scale=2 scale_sm=2 side="wide" %}

The Janxier Floodplain's Hydra used to stand in a row as a headless body and four separate snakes, which looked a lot like five enemies. Now it is one beast. A coil sits in the water with four necks rising out of it, and each head rides its own neck and bites from there.

Chop a head off and it leaves a bleeding stump. That stump won't stay empty for long. Some deaths leave a black stump instead, and the Hydra takes those personally. However many times it grows back, it never has more than four heads. It fights the way it always did. It just finally looks like one animal.

> Cut one head and two more rise, hissing and hungry; rot one at the root and the coils around the stump remember it.
>
> *The Hydra, from the Bestiary*

## The mountains, repainted

Stormwind Peaks has a new map. Muted green tundra greets you where you come in at the lower left, and the snow comes back as you climb east. The layout is the same, so every node is where you left it.

{% include figure.html src="peaks_map.webp" alt="The new Stormwind Peaks map, with green tundra and a stone stair at the lower left, a ring of standing stones, a frozen lake, a giant nest, a whirlpool of snow and a lightning-struck spire under storm clouds" caption="Stormwind Peaks" %}

The Summit's new map puts every node on its own landmark, from the snowfields and broken pillars on the left to the frost-hung temple, the ice dome, the frozen falls and the black throne at the top of the pale gold stream.

All 19 battle backgrounds across the Peaks and the Summit are new as well. Each one is the ground-level view of its node, with the landmark you saw on the map standing behind the fight. The Broken Sledge has a background of its own at last.

## Eyes in the Echoing Caverns

The Caverns map has filled out its south side, with a fungal grove, a pillared gallery, Brek's dig and a cult watch post. Shattered Gallery and Brek's Dig have moved into their new rooms, and all eleven Caverns nodes have new battle backgrounds painted from the map.

The map has company now. Every so often a pair of amber eyes opens in one of the tunnel mouths, blinks, and is gone. A bat, or a few, flits across the chasm and the dark gaps. The crystals pulse, the grove's mushrooms glow, and spores drift through it.

{% include art-strip.html items=page.art.caverns_rooms cols=3 cols_sm=3 caption="Three of the new Caverns backgrounds" %}

Troll Bridge, Shattered Gallery and Brek's Dig end on an ordinary wave now, without the boss music. They aren't boss fights, and they no longer pretend to be.

## Real art in place of plain boxes

Summon, Market Street, the inventory and the fight popups trade their plain drawn boxes for art.

- **Fight popups** dock on stone under a thin iron strap, with a red seal for Cleared and the enemies in the battle's red rings. A Genus Loci shows its own painting, and the node's painting still zooms slowly out behind it all.
- **Items** sit in dark iron wells with a glow in their rarity colour and an iron tag for the count, in your bag, on the shop shelves and in token results.
- **Summon** has a pennant, iron pips and arrows, gilt frames and a gold plate for Light 10. The recap's cards each burn their rarity's flame in an iron sconce, and the altar's candles are pixel art.
- **Market Street** has blade signs on iron brackets, stone lane plaques and nailed notices. A deal docks at the counter, and a buy that can't go through says why on the chalkboard.
- **The inventory** lays out three across on washed brick, and the item you pick opens in a panel docked in place.

Every arrow back button in the game is the same iron plate now.

## Back closes things

On Android, the phone's back button closes whatever popup, sheet or full page is on top, the same way its own X, Back or Cancel would. It never buys or confirms anything. With nothing open, Dorf goes to the background as it always has. A story tale stays put, so a stray swipe never skips the story.

## The lake, the coin and the candles

**Whisper Lake** has a camera. After the cast, the view slowly closes in on your float. Hooking a fighter punches the camera in and darkens the edges of the screen for the fight, and holding Reel shakes the lake harder as the strain builds. A caught fish pulls the view back out as it's lifted. While you wait, the float sits low in the water with little more than its red cap showing.

**Coin Toss** moves. Copper Jack's coin dips on his thumb, flies high over its shrinking shadow, lands with a rattle and a puff of dust, and slides into its notch while a fresh coin rises. Then the table shows what the flip did. Pips stamp in like ink, a broken streak rubs out, and the multiplier and score count up.

**The summon ritual** gives its best candles room to breathe. On a ten-pull the last candle burns for a full second before the recap, where it used to be cut off. A candle that turns gold gets a beat of its own, with a bigger catch, a swelling glow and the room's light swelling with it.

## Hero changes

Every Knight on the Standard banner now does his job when he spends his Valor. His Creed taunts on top of whatever it already did. Sorju's Dawn Shift already taunted, so he is unchanged. Gromm and The Grateful Dead get bigger reworks.

### Sir Gallan

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fortress Stance | Creed (needs 50 Valor): spends all Valor. Gallan takes 50% less damage and reflects 35% back to attackers for 2 turns. At 100 Valor, he is also immune to debuffs. 3 turn cooldown | Deals 80% of ATK to one enemy, then taunts all enemies for 2 turns (3 at 100 Valor). The damage cut, the reflect and the debuff immunity at 100 Valor are unchanged |

**Why:** A Knight only gets two or three turns a wave, so a turn spent on his Creed was a turn without his taunt. In test fights Fortress Stance was almost never worth casting. With the hit and the taunt, it is.

### Kensin

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Judgment of Steel | Creed (needs 50 Valor): spends all Valor. Deals 50% of ATK + 2% per Valor spent to one enemy and lowers its DEF by 20% for 2 turns. 3 turn cooldown | The same, then taunts all enemies for 2 turns. The taunt goes up even if the hit is dodged |

**Why:** Every Standard banner Knight's Creed now does his protecting job as well. Kensin's borrows the taunt from his Stand and Fight, so spending his Valor no longer means leaving the party open.
{: .why}

### Gromm

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dig In | +20% DEF for 2 turns | +20% DEF and Thorns for 4 turns. An enemy that hits him takes damage equal to 35% of his ATK |
| Shield Bash | Deals 90% of ATK to one enemy (100% at 25 Valor, 110% at 50, 120% at 75, 130% at 100) | Deals 100% of ATK (110% at 25 Valor, 120% at 50, 130% at 75, 140% at 100). Below 50% HP he still takes 20% less damage for 2 turns |
| Provoke | Taunts all enemies and gives him +10% DEF, both for 2 turns | Deals 60% of ATK to all enemies, then taunts them all and gives him +10% DEF, both for 3 turns. 1 turn cooldown |
| Not Budging | Creed (needs 50 Valor): spends all Valor. Guards one ally for 2 turns, so every hit meant for them lands on Gromm. At 100 Valor, Gromm also takes 25% less damage. 3 turn cooldown | Creed (needs 50 Valor): spends all Valor. Deals 60% of ATK + 1% per Valor spent to all enemies, then taunts them all for 2 turns. The guard and the damage cut are gone. 3 turn cooldown |

**Why:** Every hero should hold their own once fused up to 4 stars, and Gromm was the furthest behind. In test fights, parties with him were the slowest of the Standard Knights, Dig In was never worth a turn, and his spare turns went to basic attacks over Shield Bash.
{: .why}

### The Grateful Dead

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grave Tap | Deals 85% of ATK to one enemy and gains 10 Valor. At 100 Valor, 115% | The same. If the target hit him, or an ally wearing his shield, since his last turn, it also gets 25% ATK Down for 2 turns |
| A Minor Inconven&shy;ience | Needs 15 Valor to cast. Cleanses Heal Block from himself, then gives him Riposte and Provoke for 2 turns (they wore off after 1) | Works at 0 Valor. Riposte and Provoke last 3 turns |
| Graveside Manner | Needs 25 Valor to cast | Works at 0 Valor. The shields are unchanged |
| Bygone Valor | Spends all Valor (needs at least 1). Deals 60% of ATK + 1% per Valor spent (up to +100%) to all enemies and heals him for 50% of the damage dealt. Valor starts every wave at zero, so his stance and his shield sat locked early in each wave, and Bygone Valor went off at about 20 Valor, so his Valor never climbed. His stance also lasted a single enemy round.

## Also in this patch

- Error reports now carry a little play data to help balance the beta. For each play session it holds short summaries of your fights, which screens you visited and for how long, what you summoned, your gold and gems as the session went, which heroes levelled, taps on locked buttons, and your phone's make, model and Android version. There are no names, no emails and nothing you typed, only an anonymous player id. The same "Send error reports automatically" switch in Settings turns it off.
- A party leader's or a Bard's painting flares up brighter behind them.
- The tavern's gaming table shows it can be tapped, with a die that hops and tumbles now and then and a chalk GAMES slate between the stools. Either one opens the Games board.

## Fixes

- A new status icon no longer covers the one beside it. A Regen landing next to a Poison used to hide the Poison, so it looked cleansed when it wasn't.
- A hero's Riposte, Thorns, Reflect and other strike-backs now count toward their damage in the combat logs, and a Riposte that kills counts as their kill.
- The battle screen's big moves and painting flares follow the Reduce Motion setting (or your phone's), where they used to follow Reduce Transitions. Under it, a big move holds still in the middle of the screen. Whisper Lake and Coin Toss now follow the game's Reduce Motion setting too.
- In the tavern, people at the back of the tables sit on the bench instead of behind it, and the gaming table's back seats have stools.
- Tales and events leave room for their choices from the first line, so the text no longer jumps when the choices come in.
