"All things return to the earth," says Yggra. "The lent thing too." In 4.14.0 the dead don't go back alone. When a Poisoned monster falls under her lead, its Poison seeps into every monster still standing.

The Shadow King has found a better answer for anyone who hits him. They walk away Withered, and Wither now saves its worst for last.

## Rot Through the Roots

Yggra has a new leader skill. When a Poisoned enemy dies, its strongest Poison spreads to every other enemy for 2 turns. A monster that caught the rot this way passes it on when it falls, so one Poison in the right place can run through a whole wave. The rot keeps spreading after Yggra herself goes down, and an arena opponent led by her does the same to your heroes.

Her own kit feeds it. Grasping Roots poisons every enemy, Taproot holds a Poisoned enemy down for an extra turn, and Blessing of the World Root heals harder for each Poisoned enemy. Leading with Yggra is now a reason to build around Poison.

Her old leader skill, Ancient Awakening, is gone, and her ATK comes down a little. The numbers are under Hero changes.

## Wither builds

Every Wither now lasts 3 turns and gets worse as it goes. The first tick is weak, the second is twice that, and the third is three times that. Whoever casts it only decides how strong that first tick is.

A Wither landing on someone who is already Withered does nothing, so the first one always plays out. It still ignores DEF, heals still don't stop it, and a cleanse still takes it off. The icon counts down like every other effect, so a 1 means the heavy turn is next.

When the Shadow Dragon blots out the sky, the dark starts thin and thickens by the turn.

| Wither | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How long | As long as its source said | Always 3 turns |
| Damage | The same every turn | Weak, then twice that, then three times that |
| A second Wither | A stronger one replaced it, an equal one refreshed it, a weaker one fizzled | Does nothing. The first one plays out |

**Why:** Wither should read the same whoever casts it, a slow build you can see coming. Each source only sets its strength.
{: .why}

## The Shadow King stares back

{% include figure.html src="shadow-king.webp" alt="The Shadow King in spiked violet plate and a red runed tabard, swinging a great hammer trailing dark smoke" caption="The Shadow King" side=true %}

Stares Back still taunts every enemy until the end of his next turn. It no longer hurts them back. Instead, every enemy that hits him in that time, and for one turn more, is Withered. Area attacks count, since they hit him too.

Then Void Strike finishes the job. It costs less Rage now, and it goes straight through DEF only on a Withered target. Taunt, take the hits, and everyone who swung at him is open.

Cobb Rattlebag brings the same rot to the whole field. His Finale, The Sentence, now Marks and Withers every enemy, and it comes around about as often as a Wither runs out. With Cobb in the party, the Shadow King doesn't have to wait to be hit.

## Where XP goes

XP from a fight goes to the heroes who fought it. XP from everything else goes to the Library, to give to whichever hero you like. That covers an expedition's spoils, a tavern event's XP choice and the XP from map events. Those screens now say how much went to the Library, and they no longer play level-up scenes, since nobody levels there. A meal's XP bonus still counts on expeditions and map events.

**Why:** Errands and stories aren't training, so their XP shouldn't land on whoever happened to go. The Library lets you choose.
{: .why}

## Heroes on the road

A hero out on an expedition isn't locked away any more. They can take a seat in your battle party and fight in full gear while they're gone, and they can be fused and take XP. A hero already in your party can be sent on a route and stays in the party. Each hero is still on one route at a time.

**Why:** An expedition finishes on the fights you win anywhere, so keeping its heroes out of those fights only got in the way.
{: .why}

## Hero changes

### Yggra, the World Root

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base ATK | 30 | 24 |
| Leader skill | Ancient Awakening: all allies regenerate 3% of their max HP at the start of each round | Rot Through the Roots: when a Poisoned enemy dies, its strongest Poison spreads to every other enemy for 2 turns |

**Why:** She already heals the whole party with Blessing of the World Root, so a third heal made her leader skill dull, and in test fights it was worth six levels on its own. The lower ATK brings her party back into line, and her party heal still pulls its weight.
{: .why}

### Cobb Rattlebag

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Sentence (Finale) | Every enemy is Marked (+15% damage taken) and loses 10% ATK for 2 turns | Every enemy is Marked (+15% damage taken) for 2 turns and Withered for 3 turns at 25% of his ATK (25%, 50%, 75%) |

**Why:** The Sentence repeated the ATK cut his Toll the Bell already gives. Now it spreads Wither on a steady beat, and every enemy it Withers is open to the Shadow King's Void Strike.
{: .why}

### Onibaba, the Mountain Crone

| Skill | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wailing Mask | Sacrifice 20% of current HP to deal that amount as true damage to all enemies (ignores DEF). Heal all allies for 50% of damage dealt | Sacrifice 15% of current HP to deal that amount as true damage to all enemies (ignores DEF). Heal all allies for the damage dealt to one enemy |

**Why:** The heal grew with every enemy on the field. Against four enemies, each ally got back twice what she paid, and in test fights the Mask alone put her party far ahead. Now the heal is one enemy's share, so the price is real, and she pays a little less of it.
