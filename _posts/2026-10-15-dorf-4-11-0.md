"After the rains, nothing stays buried." The Janxier Floodplain has been saying it for a while, and 4.11.0 gives it more to dig up. Every node gets new waves with bigger crowds, and the Hydra finally turns up with all four heads.

Up on the Summit, the Ancient Titan has stopped being polite about it. He picks out a hero, raises both fists over them and gives you one turn to do something about that.

The battle screen gets dressed for the occasion, with iron plates for your heroes, a bronze tray for their gifts, the turn order across the top and your hero's skill book where the old button was. Casters learn some restraint too. MP still comes back every round, just never more than 15.

## The Janxier Floodplain

Every battle node on the Floodplain has new waves. Muddy Banks runs five and the rest run six, the four side paths included, which used to run three. A wave brings three to five monsters now, where it used to bring two to four, and each monster is weaker on its own to make up for the crowd.

The side paths pay for the extra work. They used to give 620 gold and 620 XP. Now each pays what the road it leaves from pays, plus a little. That's 920 at Drowned Village and Old Barrow, 970 at Peat Cuttings and 1,000 at Mill Race. Six waves should be worth the detour.

A Hydra is supposed to have four heads. Now it does. At Hydra's Lair it brings all four, and the heads do the fighting, with a Bite and a Venom Spit that hits the whole party. Kill a head with anything but a Nature skill and two grow back, up to four. Kill the body and every head dies with it.

The body used to coil up and let its heads do the work. Cut it to half health now and it stops waiting. The Hydra uncoils and joins in, and its Crushing Coil hits one hero for 150% of its ATK and stuns them.

> Cut one head and two more rise, hissing and hungry; rot one at the root and the coils around the stump remember it.
>
> The Hydra, from the Bestiary

## The Ancient Titan takes aim

The Ancient Titan on Titan's Throne has two-thirds more health than he did, and he no longer swings without warning.

When he goes for Titan's Wrath, he spends a turn raising both fists over your hero with the least health, and that hero is Singled Out. On his next turn the blow lands on them for 200% of his ATK, however much health they have by then. That gives you one turn to heal them or shield them. Stun, freeze, sleep or Silence him in between and the blow never falls at all.

He used to be a slow, patient wall. Now he gets angry. Below half health Titan's Wrath gives way to Titan's Fury, with the same warning and the same wind-up, but it hits for 250% and comes around much more often. He still calls up two Ice Wraiths at half health, so the Fury arrives with company.

To make up for it, the climb is a little kinder. Titan's Throne loses a Wind Spirit from wave 3 and an Ice Wraith from wave 5, so you reach him in better shape.

> Older than the mountains it strides across, this colossus remembers when the world was raw and the gods still bled.
>
> The Ancient Titan, from the Bestiary

## A new battle screen

The battle screen was the last part of the game still wearing the old look. Now it's redone from top to bottom.

The button you press every turn is the spine of your hero's book, with their name in gilt and a Skills label at the thumb end. The whole spine is one button, so a tap anywhere on it opens the skill menu, and the menu has a matching leather cover. This is how you reach into a fight, so it should feel that way. While a monster takes its turn your book rests shut with no name on it, and a small iron tag above it says who's acting.

Your heroes stand on iron plates, with studs at the corners in their rarity's colour. On a hero's turn their rim turns gold and a little crown sits on top.

{% include art-strip.html items=page.art.plates scale=1 scale_sm=1 caption="Hero plates by rarity, and a plate on its hero's turn" %}

Party gifts sit in a bronze tray under the round counter. Tap it to see every gift that's lit, what it does and who it's from. In the Colosseum and against rival parties, the other side's gifts get a tray of their own, marked Their gifts.

The turn order moves to a row across the top of the screen, where it's easy to read at a glance. Whoever is acting sits at the far left, bigger and framed in gold. Heroes get round blue frames and monsters get square red ones, so you can tell them apart by shape as well as colour. When someone acts they drop to the back of the line. A long line runs off the right edge, and the frames never shrink to fit.

The battle painting now fills the screen down to the bar at the bottom, and monsters stand clear of the top band, so nothing hides behind it.

## MP regen gets a ceiling

Paladins, Mages, Clerics and Druids still start every fight node with a full pool of MP, and still get 10% of it back at the start of each round. That refill now stops at 15 MP.

| Round MP regen | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every caster | 10% of max MP | 10% of max MP, at most 15 |
| With 120 max MP | 12 a round | 12 a round |
| With 300 max MP | 30 a round | 15 a round |

Up to 150 max MP nothing changes. MP from gear, buffs and the break between waves still comes in full, and Colosseum opponents play by the same rule.

**Why:** max MP grows as a hero levels while spell costs stay the same, so at high level the old refill paid for nearly any spell, every round. A big pool still buys a strong opening, and once it's spent, every cast is a choice.
