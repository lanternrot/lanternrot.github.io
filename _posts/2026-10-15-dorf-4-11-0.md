"After the rains, nothing stays buried." The Janxier Floodplain has been making that promise for a while, and in 4.11.0 the mud makes good on it. Something big has been waiting at the bottom of the road, and it brought all four of its heads this time.

Up on the Summit, the Ancient Titan has learned a new kind of cruelty. Down at the bottom of the screen, your heroes trade their old action button for a worn leather tome, iron plates under their boots, and tarnished bronze for their gifts.

## The Janxier Floodplain

Every road through the Floodplain is thicker with monsters now, and the four side paths off the main road run longer and pay out like proper expeditions.

The things in the mire have also learned to look after their own. Mud Elementals pack fresh muck into the wounds of whatever they fight beside, unless you bring something hot enough to bake them dry. Water Nagas wash their kin clean in sacred spring water, Bog Witches let the water around your party go still and sour so wounds refuse to close, and Giant Crocodiles lurk in the shallows waiting for someone to falter before they roll.

At Hydra's Lair, the Hydra finally brings all four of its heads. You know the old story about cutting a head off without the right magic to rot the neck at the root. Now even a dead stump remembers who cut it, and if you push the beast far enough, the coiled body stops letting its heads do all the work.

> Cut one head and two more rise, hissing and hungry; rot one at the root and the coils around the stump remember it.
>
> The Hydra, from the Bestiary

## The Ancient Titan takes aim

Up on Titan's Throne, the climb up the mountain is a little kinder, and the colossus waiting at the top is much harder to bring down.

He used to be a slow, patient wall that swung without warning. Now when the Ancient Titan decides one of your heroes has to die, he raises both fists high over them and lets you watch it coming. You get a single turn to figure out what to do about that before the mountain comes down. Survive long enough to make him lose his temper, and he stops being patient altogether.

> Older than the mountains it strides across, this colossus remembers when the world was raw and the gods still bled.
>
> The Ancient Titan, from the Bestiary

## A new battle screen

The battle screen was the last corner of Dorf still wearing its old frame. In 4.11.0, it is redone from top to bottom.

The button you press every turn is now the spine of your acting hero's skill book, bound in worn oxblood leather with raised bands, their name stamped in gilt, and a Skills label at the thumb end. This is how you reach into a fight, so it should feel that way. Tap anywhere along the spine to open the floor and pick a skill. While monsters take their turns, your book rests shut under a small iron tag naming who is acting. When your turn arrives, the leather lifts and your hero's name writes itself in gold.

Your party stands on forged iron plates with corner studs in their rarity's colour. On a hero's turn, their plate's rim turns gold and a small crown sits on top.

{% include art-strip.html items=page.art.plates scale=1 scale_sm=1 caption="Hero plates by rarity, and a plate on its hero's turn" %}

Above the fray, the turn order moves into a clean row across the top of the screen for a quick read at a glance. Whoever is acting sits at the far left, larger and framed in gold, followed by round blue frames for heroes and square red ones for monsters. Under the round counter, your active party gifts sit in a tarnished bronze tray. Tap it to see every lit gift, what it does, and who brought it. In the Colosseum and against rival parties, the other side's gifts get a second tray of their own.

With the old chrome gone, the battle painting fills the screen down to the book at the bottom, and monsters stand clear of the top band so nothing hides behind the interface.

## MP regen gets a ceiling

Paladins, Mages, Clerics, and Druids still start every fight node with a full pool of MP, and still recover 10% of their max MP at the start of each round. That round-start refill now stops at 15 MP.

| Round MP regen | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every caster | 10% of max MP | 10% of max MP, at most 15 |
| With 120 max MP | 12 a round | 12 a round |
| With 300 max MP | 30 a round | 15 a round |

Up to 150 max MP, nothing changes. MP from gear, skills, buffs, and the breather between waves still comes in full, and Colosseum opponents play by the same rule.

**Why:** max MP grows as a hero levels while spell costs stay flat, so at high level the old refill paid for nearly any spell every round. A big pool still buys a strong opening, and once it's spent, every cast is a choice.
